Can you trust the early years?

Before 2012 only the older MODIS sensor was flying, and it sees fewer hotspots than VIIRS for the same fire. Joined as they are, the two records make 2012 look like a fire explosion. We convert MODIS into VIIRS terms so that false step disappears.

Our harmonized recordRaw counts joined as they are

Method, exchange rates and limits
  1. Keep vegetation fires only, at nominal or high confidence, from MODIS Collection 6.1 (Terra, Aqua) and VIIRS 375 m (Suomi NPP).
  2. Remove industrial heat sources: 500 m spots VIIRS flags in 5 or more years, mostly at night.
  3. Count hotspots per month in each county and hexagon.
  4. On the overlap, February 2012 to December 2025, learn one exchange rate per calendar month: VIIRS hotspots divided by MODIS hotspots.
  5. From 2012 use VIIRS as observed. Before that, multiply the MODIS count by the rate. Before July 2002 only Terra flew, so a Terra-only rate applies.
  6. Test: predict each year from 2012 to 2025 from MODIS alone, using rates learned on the other years. The likely range comes from those errors.

Limits. The record ends in December 2025. Converted months are estimates and small areas are noisy: MODIS misses many small fires, so a converted zero can hide real burning. Satellite outages and cloud are not corrected. A hotspot is one satellite pixel that looked like fire, not a fire count or burned area.
