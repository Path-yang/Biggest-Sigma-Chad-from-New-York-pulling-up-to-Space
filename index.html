      How we made this

      Two generations of satellites watch for fire. MODIS has flown since 2000; VIIRS joined in 2012 and, with sharper eyes, sees more hotspots for the same fire. Joined as they are, the records make 2012 look like an explosion of fire. We convert the MODIS years into VIIRS terms, month by month, and test how well that works.

      The method step by step, with the exchange rates and the held-out test
      2. Count hotspots per month for the whole country, and per year in grid squares of .
      3. On the overlap, February 2012 to December 2025, learn one exchange rate per calendar month: VIIRS (Suomi NPP) hotspots divided by MODIS hotspots, pulled toward the country's all-month rate, and that toward a global rate, where the country has few hotspots.
      4. From February 2012 use VIIRS as observed. Before that, multiply the MODIS count by the rate. Before July 2002 only Terra flew, so a Terra-only rate applies.
      5. Test: predict each year from 2012 to 2025 from MODIS alone, using rates learned on the other years. The 90% range comes from those held-out errors and is wider for small counts.
      6. NOAA-20 VIIRS is shown for comparison only. On those days NOAA-20's count is multiplied by the country's NOAA-20-to-Suomi NPP rate, learned on 2018–2025, and the month gets a 90% range.

      Limits. The record ends in December 2025. Converted months are estimates and small countries are noisy: MODIS misses many small fires, so a converted zero can hide real burning. Satellite outages and cloud are not corrected. A hotspot is one satellite pixel that looked like fire, not a fire count or burned area.

        Can you trust the early years?

        Before 2012 only the older MODIS sensor was flying, and it sees fewer hotspots than VIIRS for the same fire. Joined as they are, the two records make 2012 look like a fire explosion. We convert MODIS into VIIRS terms so that false step disappears.

        Our harmonized recordRaw counts joined as they are

        Method, exchange rates and limits
        1. Keep vegetation fires only, at nominal or high confidence, from MODIS Collection 6.1 (Terra, Aqua) and VIIRS 375 m (Suomi NPP).
        2. Remove industrial heat sources: 500 m spots VIIRS flags in 5 or more years, mostly at night.
        3. Count hotspots per month in each county and hexagon.
        4. On the overlap, February 2012 to December 2025, learn one exchange rate per calendar month: VIIRS hotspots divided by MODIS hotspots.
        5. From 2012 use VIIRS as observed. Before that, multiply the MODIS count by the rate. Before July 2002 only Terra flew, so a Terra-only rate applies.
        6. Test: predict each year from 2012 to 2025 from MODIS alone, using rates learned on the other years. The likely range comes from those errors.
        7. On those days NOAA-20's count is multiplied by the state's NOAA-20-to-Suomi NPP ratio for the month, from 2018–2025, and the month gets a likely range.
        8. 2026 comes from the FIRMS API: standard processing to July, near real time from August. Near-real-time rows have no type flag, so a point within 0.01° of a past or 2026 non-vegetation detection counts as one, and the 2012–2025 industrial mask applies unchanged.

        Limits. The record runs to 14 November 2026, the last complete day. 2026 is a partial year: its total is “so far”, it is not ranked against whole years, November 2026 is compared only with 1–14 November of past years, and later months are blank, not zero. MODIS was not fetched for 2026. Converted months are estimates and small areas are noisy: MODIS misses many small fires, so a converted zero can hide real burning. Suomi NPP outages from 2018 on are filled from NOAA-20 the same way as in 2026; earlier gaps and cloud are not corrected. A hotspot is one satellite pixel that looked like fire, not a fire count or burned area.
